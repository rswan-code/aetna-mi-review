You asked us to improve access to care for pre/post natal members. In 7 months, Pyx has enrolled 479 Aetna Michigan members — with 89% engaging directly with the care team (vs. 68% under the prior model) — and translated that engagement into a 56% care visit completion rate among engaged members.

Enrollment reflects priority populations aligned to plan goals: pregnancy cohort, high-cost/high-utilizer, maternal health support, and target diagnosis. Every enrolled member is in active engagement cadence with a dedicated care team member.

NEW MODEL — PROACTIVE, NEEDS-BASED

22 minutes per call vs. prior model — depth of conversation is what surfaces barriers and drives action

The shift to a proactive, needs-based model changed the nature of engagement. Onboarding now centers on identifying member needs upfront, which informs a care plan and guides ongoing outreach. The result: 89% of enrolled members engage directly with the care team — 21 percentage points above the prior model — and calls run more than twice as long. That depth is what uncovers barriers that standard outreach misses and builds the trust that moves members to care.

Talking Points — Member Engagement

  • 89% care team engagement vs. 68% under the prior model — this is not a marginal improvement. It reflects a structural shift: the proactive, needs-based model gets more members into a sustained engagement relationship with the care team
  • 22 average minutes per call (+147%) means every call is a substantive conversation. At that depth, care team members are uncovering barriers, reviewing care needs, and building the trust that moves members to action
  • +70% increase in overall engagement since the model change reflects the compounding effect of deeper, more intentional outreach. The activity metrics reflect a qualitatively different program than what ran before
  • 16 avg app activities per member alongside 89% care team engagement shows both channels are active — the model is not app-dependent, but members who engage with the app are completing meaningful support activities at scale

CRITICAL FINDING — 75–84 COHORT

UCLA-3 of 6.0 + PHQ-4 of 7.0 — compound clinical risk at the loneliness threshold in the oldest cohort

The 75–84 age group reaches the clinical loneliness threshold (UCLA-3 ≥6) and registers moderate distress on the PHQ-4 (score 7.0, in the moderate 6–8 range) — the only cohort where both scores compound at clinical severity simultaneously. These members are invisible in standard claims data. Pyx is the identification layer for a risk profile that predicts higher utilization, missed appointments, and behavioral health escalation.

Talking Points — Loneliness & Distress Screening

  • Overall UCLA-3 of 4.9 indicates a meaningfully lonely population — below the clinical threshold of 6 but approaching it, with older cohorts already there. The 75–84 group is at 6.0, at the threshold
  • PHQ-4 of 3.9 overall sits at the lower boundary of mild distress — but the 75–84 cohort at 7.0 is in moderate distress territory. These are members at elevated risk for behavioral health utilization if not engaged
  • The compound risk in the 75–84 cohort (UCLA-3 6.0 + PHQ-4 7.0) represents a clinical profile no claims file surfaces. Pyx identifies these members through sustained engagement and routes them to elevated care team attention — and to behavioral health care access
  • 528 UCLA-3 and 470 PHQ-4 screenings represent a clinical baseline dataset for the enrolled population — the foundation for tracking loneliness and distress trajectory over the full program tenure

Aetna Michigan's access improvement is happening where the state needs it most

Michigan faces significant maternal health access challenges — rural coverage gaps, transportation barriers (the fastest-rising identified barrier in this program at 40%), and health literacy gaps that prevent members from navigating a complex care system. Aetna Michigan's increase in access to prenatal and postpartum care shows movement where access is most limited. The 86% maternal member visit completion rate is not just an engagement metric — it is clinical access being created in a state that needs it.
